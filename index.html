01 — ABOUT
HUMAN MOVEMENT COMES FIRST.
The phenomenon I study is not the data.

It is human movement.

I am a physiotherapist and PhD researcher in Rehabilitation Sciences, working with human movement through a biomechanical and computational perspective.

My background in physiotherapy shapes the way I approach movement: not simply as a collection of signals, but as a representation of how the human body moves, adapts and produces mechanical responses.

I work across clinical reasoning, experimental measurement, biomechanical modelling, computational analysis and interpretation.

06 — RESEARCH
PhD · Rehabilitation Sciences · Human Movement

Asking mechanical
questions about
human movement.

How does the
foot contribute
to movement?

My doctoral research investigates the biomechanical contribution of the midfoot to body acceleration during walking and running.

My work combines motion capture, force measurement, biomechanical modelling and computational analysis to understand how local joint mechanics can influence movement at a distance.

This research reflects the broader question that guides my work: how can technology and biomechanics help us better understand human movement?
